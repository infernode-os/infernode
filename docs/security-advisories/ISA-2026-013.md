## Summary

A review of the agent confinement stack found several ways out of the
grants it made:

- path policy compared names exactly while macOS and Windows hosts fold
  case, so a denied path spelt in another case was allowed; bare
  `/chan`, `/fd`, `/usr`, keyring paths and numbered aliases of
  `/mnt/toolctl` and `/tool` were not refused;
- a parent's grant of a root authorised a child path the parent itself
  could not see, and a `/usr` grant did not mask keyring and secstore
  credentials;
- a confined `NODEVS` process could follow a host symlink out of a
  granted tree;
- mc9p let one client read or replace another client's response, and
  its `fs` domain took paths outside its `-r` directory, through host
  symlinks too;
- the approval gate could be satisfied by delivered task or message
  events or by conversational text, not only by the operator's `Allow`;
- a confined command could survive its deadline by starting a new
  process group.

## Impact

A model, or text that steered it, could read or write files outside
its grants (credentials included) and have gated actions approved
without the operator.

## Fix

Privileged path policy is in one place and compares names as the host
does; parents delegate only what they can see; `/usr` grants mask
credentials; `NODEVS` processes do not follow host symlinks out of a
tree; mc9p responses are per client and its `fs` domain is held to its
root under `NODEVS`; only the exact `Allow` action approves a call; the
exec tool kills the whole process tree (`killtree`).

## Workarounds

None. Update to 0.10.0 or later.

Found by the project's security review.
