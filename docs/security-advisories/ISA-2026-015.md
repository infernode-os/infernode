## Summary

On the bare-metal kernel, a board with no working hardware generator
(a Pi 4 whose RNG200 did not answer, a PolarFire whose nonce service
refused or stopped after boot, a QEMU virt without virtio-rng) gave
libsec's key generators, `#c/random` and `#c/notquiterandom` a counter
stirred with the time, after one line on the console (none on the
PolarFire). Separately, the Pi 3 and Pi 4 drivers tested for a word and
read it without a lock, so two cores could take the same word, and the
RNG200's own health-test failure was never read.

## Impact

Keys, nonces and secrets made on such a board (signer and node keys,
session keys, login exchanges) could be predicted from the time of
boot. The hosted emulator, which uses the host's generator, was not
affected.

## Fix

The hardware generator never makes bytes up; key generation and
`/dev/random` wait for real bytes and say why every five seconds. The
Pi's generators are read under a lock and the RNG200 is reset when its
health test fails. 0.10.1 then put one SP 800-90A HMAC_DRBG, seeded from
the hardware under SP 800-90B's health tests, behind every reader.

## Workarounds

On a board that ran a release before 0.10.0, if its console ever said
the hardware generator was missing, make its keys again after updating.

Found internally (INFR-404).
