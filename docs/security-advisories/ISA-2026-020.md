## Summary

`slhdsa192s` and `slhdsa256s` took the WOTS+ checksum's three base-16
digits from the bottom of its sixteen bits instead of the top (FIPS 205
Algorithm 7), so the checksum's top digit was dropped and what was
signed was the checksum mod 256. The checksum is what stops a WOTS+
signature being chained forward; without its top digit, a message whose
digits are all at least the signed one's and sum to 256 more had the
same checksum digits, and its signature followed from the first using
only public hashing. Every layer of the hypertree signs the root below
it this way. Three other departures from FIPS 205 made the signatures
non-standard without weakening them.

## Impact

Someone who had seen enough signatures from an SLH-DSA key (a signer
made with `createsignerkey -a slhdsa192s` and the certificates it
issued, or an audit log's checkpoints) could work towards signing
material of their own under it; the review put the cost for 192s within
reach of ordinary hardware after some hundreds of signatures. A host
test chains a WOTS+ signature forward to another message, which the old
code accepted ten times in ten.

## Fix

Signing follows FIPS 205 (checked against OpenSSL and a reference in
Python); signatures and certificates made by the old code are refused.
Keys are unchanged.

## Workarounds

None. Update to 0.11.0 or later and have a signer with an SLH-DSA key
issue its certificates again; do not trust anything signed before on
the strength of its old signature.

Found by the project's security review.
