## Summary

`ethcrypto`'s `strtoaddr` removed a `0x` and handed the rest to
`hexdecode`, which removed another: `0x0x` and 38 hex digits, 42
characters like an address, came back as 19 bytes. wallet9p listed such
a recipient for approval as written and, once approved, wrote the 19
bytes into the transfer's calldata with a zero after them. For an x402
authorization, the EIP-712 encoder turned anything not 20 bytes into
the zero address, which the wallet then signed a transfer to.

## Impact

An agent, or a payment-requesting (HTTP 402) server, could have an
approved payment go to an address nobody was shown, or to `0x00...00`,
where the tokens are lost.

## Fix

An address is exactly `0x` and forty hex digits, with its EIP-55
checksum checked when it has mixed case; the EIP-712 encoders refuse a
field that is not what its type says; the approver is shown the
recipient as formed from the bytes that will be used.

## Workarounds

On a release before 0.11.0, approve no payment whose recipient is not
exactly 42 characters with one `0x` prefix.

Found by the project's security review.
